Onboarding the new release engineer to the storefront platform

Welcome to the team. The following document walks you through everything you need to do during your first two weeks so you have a clear, well grounded sense of how the storefront platform works in practice. Please do not feel that you need to rush through any of this; the team has deliberately allocated time for onboarding and we would rather have you take an extra day on a section that is unfamiliar than skim it and run into a surprise in your first on call rotation.

Step one is to confirm that all of your accounts are working. You will receive an email from the IT team within twenty four hours of your start date that contains the credentials and links you need. Please log into each of the following systems and confirm that you can read and write where appropriate: the source code repository, the issue tracker, the chat platform, the email platform, the documentation portal, the metrics platform, the logging platform, the on call paging tool, and the deploy console. If any of these do not work, please send a short message to the IT support channel and someone will help you within the same business day.

Step two is to set up your local development environment. The recommended setup is documented in the platform setup guide, which lives in the developer portal under the platform section. Please follow the steps in the guide exactly as written, even if you have a strong preference for a different shell or editor. The goal of the standard setup is not to dictate your preferences but to ensure that when you ask for help, the person helping you is working with the same tools and configuration as you are. Once you have completed the standard setup, you are welcome to layer your personal preferences on top.

Step three is to read the architectural overview document. The overview is a high level walkthrough of how the storefront platform is organized at the level of services, queues, databases, and external integrations. It is intentionally not deep on any one topic; the goal is to give you a mental map you can use to orient yourself when you encounter a specific component for the first time. The document is approximately forty pages long and we expect that reading it carefully will take most of your second day. Please write down any questions you have as you read; we will go through them together at the end of your first week.

Step four is to shadow a deploy. The platform deploys multiple times per business day, and you will be invited to a deploy session within your first week. Your job during the shadow is not to drive anything; your job is to watch carefully and ask questions. Pay particular attention to the moments when the deploy engineer pauses, checks a dashboard, and confirms that something looks healthy before continuing. Those moments are where the institutional knowledge of the team lives.

Step five is to work through the platform katas. The katas are a set of small, deliberately simple exercises that you complete in a sandbox environment. Each kata exercises one part of the platform, such as opening a database connection, publishing a message to a queue, or invoking an internal service. The katas are scaffolded so you can complete them in any order, but we recommend starting with the database kata because it gives you the most useful intuition for how the system holds state.

Step six is to pair with a senior engineer on a real ticket. This typically happens in your second week. The ticket will be chosen so it is small enough to complete in two days but real enough that it exercises the full path from understanding the requirement to landing the change in production. You will not be expected to lead the work; you will be expected to ask questions, follow along, and produce a pull request at the end with the senior engineer reviewing.

Step seven is to take your first solo ticket. By the end of your second week you should have enough context to pick up a small, well scoped ticket from the backlog and ship it without pairing. The expectation is that you will reach out for help whenever you are unsure about anything; there is no expectation that you will know everything on your own. The team prefers a thoughtful question early to a confident mistake late.

Throughout the first two weeks, please use the daily standup to surface anything that is blocking you. The team is small and we will reorganize around removing your blockers when we hear about them. We would much rather you tell us you are stuck for the third day in a row than struggle silently for a week. Please also remember to take breaks, take lunch away from your desk, and finish at a reasonable hour. Sustainable pace is the only pace that ships.
