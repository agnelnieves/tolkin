The lighthouse keeper checked the lamp twice before sunset, the way he had every evening for eleven years. Fog was rolling in from the south, thick enough to swallow the harbor lights one by one. He wrote the time in the logbook, poured a cup of tea, and climbed the spiral stairs to the gallery.

From the top he could see the last fishing boat turning toward the breakwater. The radio crackled with a weather advisory: small craft warnings through morning, seas building to six feet. He acknowledged the bulletin, adjusted the rotation timer, and settled into the long quiet hours of the watch. Routine, he often said, is just another word for care repeated until it looks easy.
